\section*{Introduction}
This report documents the progress on SPICE exercises based on the problem sets suggested by Prof. Sunil Khatri (Texas A\&M). The objective is to develop thorough proficiency with SPICE simulation tools prior to PhD research. The key learnings and simulation results are presented below, with primary focus on Cadence Spectre.


\section{Exercise 1: CMOS Inverter Chain}
A 7-stage CMOS inverter chain was simulated using the 22-nm PTM HP model.

\subsection{Initial Smoke Test ($k=2$)}
For the initial smoke test, the following parameters were used: $L = 22\text{ nm}$, $W_n = 44\text{ nm}$, $W_p = 88\text{ nm}$, and $V_{DD} = 1\text{ V}$. Propagation delays were measured graphically at the 50\% $V_{DD}$ crossing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $t_{PHL} \approx 1.9286\text{ ps}$
    \item $t_{PLH} \approx 2.668\text{ ps}$
\end{itemize}
Clearly, $t_{PLH} > t_{PHL}$.

    \centering
    \begin{minipage}{0.45\textwidth}

    \begin{minipage}{0.45\textwidth}

        \caption{$t_{PLH}$ measurement for $k=2$.}
    \end{minipage}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Part A: Sweep of $k$}
To determine the PMOS-to-NMOS width ratio ($k$) at which $t_{PHL} = t_{PLH}$, $k$ was swept from 1 to 2 in steps of 0.1.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{SPECTRE/Exercise1/tplh_vs_tphl_as_a_function_of_k.png}
    \caption{Sweep result showing $t_{PLH}$ and $t_{PHL}$ as a function of $k$.}
\end{figure}

From the sweep data, the curves intersect at $k \approx 1.293$, where $t_{PHL} \approx t_{PLH} \approx 2.13\text{ ps}$. This was verified using the Spectre calculator utility, which yielded the same intersection point.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{SPECTRE/Exercise1/k_value_for_tplh_equal_tphl_from_calc_utility.png}
    \caption{Spectre calculator utility confirming $k \approx 1.293$.}
\end{figure}

The rise and fall times ($t_{rise}$ and $t_{fall}$) were also measured over the same $k$ sweep. These two quantities intersect at a different point than the propagation delays.

    \centering
    \begin{minipage}{0.45\textwidth}

\end{figure}

The rise and fall times become equal at $k \approx 1.82$, where $t_{rise} \approx t_{fall} \approx 4.55\text{ ps}$.

\subsection{Part B: Sweep of $X$ for Symmetric Rise/Fall Times}
To find the input transition time ($X$) at which $t_{rise} = t_{fall}$, $X$ was varied while keeping $k$ fixed, and both rise and fall times were measured.

Using the symmetric $X \approx 6.145\text{ ps}$ obtained from Part B, $k$ was swept from 1 to 10 in steps of 0.05 to re-examine both the propagation delays and the rise/fall times.

\section{Exercise 2: Ring Oscillator}
A ring oscillator was simulated using the 22-nm PTM HP model, with the symmetric delay ratio $k = 1.293$ obtained from Exercise 1. The design parameters were $L = 22\text{ nm}$, $W_n = 44\text{ nm}$, and $V_{DD} = 1\text{ V}$.

\subsection{Part A: 21-Stage Ring Oscillator}
A 21-stage ring oscillator was constructed by feeding the output of inverter 21 back to the input of inverter 1.

\subsubsection{SPICE \texttt{.measure} Results}
The Spectre \texttt{.measure} directive yielded:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $T_{\text{period}} = 89.385\text{ ps}$
    \item $t_{PHL} = 2.1287\text{ ps}$
    \item $t_{PLH} = 2.1287\text{ ps}$
\end{itemize}
As expected, $t_{PHL} \approx t_{PLH}$ since $k = 1.293$.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{SPECTRE/Exercise2/SPICE_MEASURMENT_OF_T_PERIOD_TPHL_TPLH.png}
    \caption{Spectre \texttt{.measure} output for the 21-stage ring oscillator.}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Graphical Measurements}
Propagation delays and the oscillation period were also measured graphically at the 50\% $V_{DD}$ crossing:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $t_{PHL} \approx 776.4239 - 774.2987 = 2.125\text{ ps}$
    \item $t_{PLH} \approx 2.13\text{ ps}$
    \item $T = 863.6816 - 774.2987 = 89.383\text{ ps}$
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Python Verification}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{SPECTRE/Exercise2/PYTHON_TRANSIENT_ANALYSIS_FOR_TPLH_TPHL_TPERIOD.png}
    \caption{Python-generated transient analysis confirming $t_{PLH}$, $t_{PHL}$, and $T$.}
\end{figure}

The Python verification yielded $t_{PLH} \approx 2.18\text{ ps}$, $t_{PHL} \approx 2.13\text{ ps}$, and $T \approx 89.38\text{ ps}$, in close agreement with the Spectre results.

\subsubsection{Summary}
The oscillation period is:
\[
    \boxed{T = 89.385\text{ ps}} \qquad f_{\text{osc}} = \frac{1}{T} \approx 11.19\text{ GHz}
\]
The theoretical relationship $T = 2N \cdot t_p = 42 \cdot t_p$ was verified:
\[
    42 \times 2.1287 = 89.405\text{ ps} \approx T_{\text{measured}} = 89.385\text{ ps} \quad \checkmark
\]
