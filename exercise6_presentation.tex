% ---- Box 11 : Reduction Ratios & Physical Discussion ----
\slidebox{11}{
    \centering
    \textbf{Leakage Reduction Ratios \textemdash{} $I_{leak}^\text{(A)} \,/\, I_{leak}^\text{(X)}$}\\[0.15cm]
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{@{}c r r r@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Input (AB)} & \textbf{A\,/\,B} & \textbf{A\,/\,C} & \textbf{A\,/\,D} \\
        \midrule
        00 &          $7.4\times$ &         $4.8\times$ &           $63\times$ \\
        01 &        $134\times$   &       $103\times$   &       $1{,}600\times$ \\
        10 &        $531\times$   &       $372\times$   &       $8{,}900\times$ \\
        11 & $\mathbf{4{,}300\times}$ & $\mathbf{1{,}070\times}$ & $\mathbf{118{,}800\times}$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}\\[0.2cm]
    \flushleft\scriptsize
    $\bullet$\;\textbf{AB\,=\,11 is worst-case:} Both NMOS ON $\Rightarrow$ low-$R$ pull-down; full $V_{DD}$ appears
    across the two OFF PMOS devices, maximising sub-threshold leakage.\\[0.04cm]
    $\bullet$\;\textbf{Header $>$ footer at AB\,=\,11:} The dominant leakage path pulls current from $V_{DD}$ through the
    OFF PMOS pair.  A supply-side HVT header directly strangles this path ($4{,}300\times$ vs.\
    footer's $1{,}070\times$).\\[0.04cm]
    $\bullet$\;\textbf{B \& C flatten state dependence:} A single HVT device becomes the bottleneck,
    capping $I_{DD}$ to its own sub-threshold current regardless of NAND-core state
    (B spans only $3\times$ vs.\ A's $200\times$).\\[0.04cm]
    $\bullet$\;\textbf{D = multiplicative suppression:} Leakage must tunnel through \emph{two} HVT barriers,
    yielding up to $\sim\!5$ orders of magnitude reduction.
}
